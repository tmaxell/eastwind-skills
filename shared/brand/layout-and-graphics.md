# Layout, graphics, and photography

Source: `Brand Guidelines_L.pdf`, sections 05 and 06.

## The mood the design has to carry

The brand archetype is the **sage**, in the guise of a benevolent expert and
mentor, with the **magician** as a secondary archetype — the professional who
turns old experience into new results. The visual consequence is stated plainly in
the guideline: the design is laconic, the tone calm. The company does not have to
prove its competence; the products do that.

The sage buyer wants a logical description and considers themselves informed.
They are drawn to noble, high-quality design with a trace of "elitism" — not gold
ornament, but the elitism of a closed club whose codes only insiders read.

Beacon words for the mood: thought, knowledge, evidence, objectivity, research,
insight, figure, report. Rational, logical, competent, conceptual, scientific.

Practically: **do not overload the space. Always leave enough air. Keep to
minimalism.**

## Layout principles

Anything that looks logical and rational: modular grid, block layout, careful
alignment. Layouts must be informative and useful — no flourish, no decoration.
Encoding a rebus or a puzzle in the layout, playing with imagination, or a thin
witty joke are all in character.

Named directions the guideline endorses:

- **Swiss style** — functional, neutral, universal.
- **Framing** — table-like, a composition that is logical and verified.
- **Neomorphism and UI graph systems** — technological, progressive.
- **Stezaker-style image play** — building new meaning out of found images.

## Forms

Start from the geometry of the logo: circle, square, four-sided polygon, triangle.
Precise geometry gives layouts clarity — squares, hexagons, triangles, ellipses.
Use arrows actively; the arrow underlies the logo. Straight lines, schemes,
charts, and diagrams all fit. Stay geometric and abstract; avoid the shapes of
concrete objects.

## Textures

Ascetic textures of concrete and various papers. Cold marble, granite, plaster.
Checkerboard and other black-and-white patterns that fold into optical illusions
and make the brain work.

## The three graphic styles

Pick one of the three per artifact.

**1. Illustrative.** The rule is to work with metaphor: the forms must express the
meaning of what is being illustrated. Eastwind mostly deals in abstract concepts
with no physical analogue, and the graphics should underline that. Good subjects:
mazes of lines; schemes and schematic drawings with icons; blueprints or drawings
that resemble them; geometric images; hard rhythmic repetition.

**2. Primitives.** Illustration built out of primitive shapes.

**3. Generative.** Abstract images generated by a neural network in saturated
colors, or images in a flat style.

Linear illustrations resembling schematics or blueprints, and surrealist
illustration in the manner of Escher or Fukuda, both fit the archetype.

### Never, in graphics

- Dark theme.
- Photographs with concrete objects or images inside the graphic.
- Any historical or contemporary style — cyberpunk, steampunk, Matrix-like
  imagery.
- Imitating neon or glow in neural-network output.
- Meaningless ornament in the vein of Bauhaus or Neo-Geo.

## Photography

Any type of photograph is allowed — portrait, reportage, abstraction. The general
rule: light tones, soft shadows, dispassion, calm, intellect.

- People shot as if for a passport: open gaze, calm expression.
- Reportage must be documentary, not artistic.
- Photographs must look natural and unstaged. Avoid model-posed shots.
- Keep the frame clean; avoid overloading it.
- Do not apply photo styles or filters. Set the white balance on white.
- People may be cut out from the background for posts or advertising.

### Composition

Turn on the camera's 3×3 grid. Although the brand grid is the 8× module, photo
composition follows the classical golden-section rules and the tool at hand.

- Place the main subject — object or person — on one of the intersections.
  Prefer the right-hand points.
- Shoot at eye level. Avoid low and high angles.
- Leave enough air to the left and right of the subject.

### Never, in photography

- Dark theme.
- Tinted photos; bright, staged colors.
- Clichés and collages, excessive decoration, obvious retouching.
- Macro, and excessive background blur.

## Text and graphics over photographs

- Do not place text or graphics on a photograph.
- Do not tint a photograph in any brand color or gradient.
- Avoid staged bright objects or accents in the frame.
- Do not crop through the compositional center — people or objects.
- When material must sit on a photograph, put the logo on a plate.
- Always work from the modular grid.
- Work in the light theme by preference.
